\section{Context and Motivation}\label{sec:intro-context}
\begin{itemize}
    \item ML-based NIDS now outperform signature-based systems on benchmark datasets (CIC-IDS2017, CIC-DDoS-2019, Kitsune) but inherit a new attack surface: adversarial evasion.
    \item Real attackers are \textbf{black-box} (query-only) and \textbf{protocol-constrained} --- the Inverse Feature Mapping (IFM) problem: a feature-space perturbation must correspond to a \emph{realizable} packet/flow.
    \item Most prior defenses assume unrealistic threat models (white-box gradients) or train against feature-space perturbations that cannot exist as real traffic.
    \item TANTGen (sibling thesis) closes that gap on the \emph{attack} side --- a black-box, protocol-compliant, Transformer-based adversarial traffic generator. ADEPT is the \emph{defense} answer, trained and evaluated against exactly that realistic threat, not a synthetic one.
\end{itemize}

% #############################################################################
\section{Problem Statement}\label{sec:intro-problem}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Given a streaming flow of packets that may be benign, naturally malicious (e.g.\ a DDoS), or adversarially perturbed (TANTGen-style evasion of an upstream NIDS): detect the adversarial manipulation \textbf{as early as possible} and \textbf{neutralize} it before it reaches the protected NIDS --- without degrading clean-traffic accuracy.
    \item Key constraints: streaming (no full-flow buffering), black-box realism, protocol compliance of any ``fixed'' output, and adaptability to a generator that itself evolves.
\end{itemize}

\section{Work Objectives and Challenges}\label{sec:intro-objectives}
\begin{itemize}
    \item O1 --- Feature representation: a packet/flow representation usable by heterogeneous learners at different temporal granularities (nPrint bit-level + IPAT + Direction).
    \item O2 --- Ensemble-based detection: combine architecturally diverse base learners (differentiable vs.\ non-differentiable) to raise the cost of transferable gradient attacks.
    \item O3 --- Input purification: reconstruct a ``denoised'' version of a flagged flow that lands back on the benign manifold while remaining a valid, realizable packet sequence.
    \item O4 --- Continuous adaptation: a reinforcement-learning component closes the loop so the defense keeps adapting to an evolving attacker rather than staying static.
    \item O5 --- Comprehensive, streaming-aware evaluation (not just end-of-flow accuracy).
    \item Challenges: extreme class/source imbalance (reflection floods dominate flow counts); near-invisibility of pure-timing perturbations (\texttt{iat} mode); latency budget at 100\,Gbps+.
\end{itemize}

\section{Rationale and Contributions}\label{sec:intro-contributions}
\subsection{Rationale}\label{subsec:intro-rationale}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Why stacking over a single architecture: gradient-based perturbations crafted against a differentiable model transfer poorly to a non-differentiable one (RF) --- a ``rugged'' decision boundary.
    \item Why a Transformer purifier over GAN/AutoEncoder: causal/sequential structure of packet streams; KV-cache-friendly decoding for latency.
    \item Why an adaptive (RL) component over a static defense: static defenses are eventually learned and bypassed; an adaptive loop lets the defense keep moving.
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Contributions}\label{subsec:intro-contrib-list}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A streaming-native packet-to-tensor pipeline unifying dense (TCN) and sparse (RF) prediction cadences over the same nPrint-based representation.
    \item A heterogeneous RF+TCN ensemble fused by an attention-based meta-learner, evaluated on a like-for-like streaming axis (AUROC@t, EDP), not just end-of-flow metrics.
    \item A causal purification Transformer achieving (measured) a 7.5$\times$ MSE reduction and a 0.000 residual detection gap on strongly-perturbed sources, with \texttt{tshark}-verified protocol compliance on realized PCAPs.
    \item A reinforcement-learning feedback loop that lets the defense adapt online to an evolving adversary.
    \item A 10-source, 3-perturbation-mode evaluation corpus (Kitsune + CIC-IDS2017 + CIC-DDoS-2019 $\times$ TANTGen) reusable for future ADEPT-class defense work.
\end{itemize}
