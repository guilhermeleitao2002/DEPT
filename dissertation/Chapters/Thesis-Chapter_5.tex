This chapter evaluates DEPT against the threat model of \Cref{sec:sol-threat}: a
black-box, protocol-compliant adversary, concretely the TANTGen generator of
\Cref{sec:sol-pipeline} treated as an opaque red team, that manipulates a streaming
flow so as to evade a downstream NIDS. The chapter first fixes the research questions
(\Cref{sec:eval-rqs}), the corpus and environment (\Cref{sec:eval-setup}), and the
metric definitions (\Cref{sec:eval-metrics}). It then reports the two base learners
(\Cref{sec:eval-base}), their attention-based fusion (\Cref{sec:eval-meta}), and the
purification stage (\Cref{sec:eval-purification}), before turning to the end-to-end
system (\Cref{sec:eval-e2e}) and to an explicit accounting of the limitations
(\Cref{sec:eval-discussion}). Throughout, the results are reported as measured,
including the sources and perturbation modes on which DEPT does poorly.

\section{Research Questions}\label{sec:eval-rqs}

The evaluation is organized around two research questions, each tied to one of the
work objectives of \Cref{sec:intro-objectives}:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (ensemble robustness).} Does heterogeneous stacking, a
    non-differentiable Random Forest fused with a differentiable Temporal Convolutional
    Network, measurably raise the cost of a transferable, gradient-guided evasion attack
    relative to a single-architecture baseline?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (purification effectiveness).} Does the reconstructor move a
    flagged adversarial flow back onto the benign manifold, closing the detection gap
    seen by the protected NIDS, while emitting a realizable, protocol-compliant packet
    sequence?
\end{itemize}

Both questions are answered by measurement. RQ2 is answered through the
reconstructor's own metrics (\Cref{sec:eval-purification}) and, end to end, through the
deployment baseline that scores the defense against the independent \ac{NIDS} each
attack was built to evade (\Cref{sec:eval-e2e}).

RQ1 needs an adversary TANTGen cannot supply. TANTGen is black-box and gradient-free by
design, so it prices no gradient path and cannot say what heterogeneity is worth against
one; taking its success rate as evidence for RQ1 would answer a question about gradients
with an attacker that has none. \Cref{sec:eval-gradablation} therefore introduces a second,
complementary adversary that differentiates the \ac{TCN} directly and whose perturbation
is then scored, unchanged, against the Random Forest, the \ac{TCN}, and the fused stack.
The asymmetry is the point: the attacker has an exact gradient into one half of the
ensemble and none into the other, which is the situation RQ1 describes.

A third natural question, whether the detect-then-purify pipeline stays within a
real-time inference budget, is deliberately not addressed here: the wall-clock latency
and overhead evaluation of the delivered system is left to future work
(\Cref{chap:conclusion}).

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:eval-setup}

\subsection{Datasets and Threat Generation}\label{subsec:eval-datasets}

The evaluation corpus combines ten benign traffic sources (Mirai and the SSDP flood
from Kitsune; DDoS, Port Scan and Brute Force from CIC-IDS2017; and the five
CIC-DDoS-2019 reflection and amplification floods, namely NTP, DNS, SSDP, SYN, and
LDAP), each paired with three TANTGen adversarial variants: \texttt{both} (timing and
size), \texttt{iat} (inter-arrival timing only), and \texttt{size} (payload/size
only). The per-source detection tables of this chapter therefore cover ten sources and
thirty source--mode combinations. Under
DEPT's label convention (\Cref{sec:sol-pipeline}), every raw-capture flow
is labelled \emph{benign}, since the upstream NIDS is responsible for the datasets'
native attacks, and every TANTGen-generated flow is labelled \emph{adversarial}. The
task DEPT is measured on is therefore precise: distinguish a flow that TANTGen has
manipulated from one it has not, and undo that manipulation. This framing is a
deliberate choice, and its consequences (in particular, that a source on which TANTGen
barely perturbs the features is essentially unlabelable) are analysed in
\Cref{sec:eval-discussion}.

The corpus as tensorized comprises $8{,}624{,}958$ flows and $61{,}516{,}406$ packets
across the forty sources (ten benign and thirty adversarial). All models share a single
flow-atomic, source-stratified $70/15/15$ split (seed~$42$), so that the RF, TCN,
meta-learner, and reconstructor are trained, validated, and tested on the same flows and
their results are directly comparable. Because the CIC-DDoS-2019 reflection floods
contribute the overwhelming majority of adversarial flows, the held-out test pool is
adversarial-dominated: of the $1{,}293{,}737$ test flows seen by the TCN and
meta-learner, $1{,}082{,}586$ ($83.7\%$) are adversarial and $211{,}151$ are benign. The
RF, whose first checkpoint fires at packet~$2$, never sees the $6{,}179$ single-packet
flows and therefore scores $1{,}287{,}558$ flows on the streaming axis, or
$1{,}838{,}798$ rows on its native per-checkpoint axis. This base rate matters when
reading accuracy and precision, and it is the reason the cross-model comparison is
anchored on threshold-free, base-rate-robust quantities (AUROC and PR~AUC) and on the
streaming axis rather than on raw accuracy.

\subsection{Implementation Environment}\label{subsec:eval-environment}

All training and evaluation were run on a single research server: $2\times$ Intel Xeon
Gold~5320 CPUs @~$2.2$\,GHz ($52$ physical cores, $104$ hardware threads), $160$\,GB of
RAM, and $2\times$ NVIDIA RTX~6000~Ada GPUs, of which one was used at a time. The
software stack is the one described in \Cref{sec:sol-tools}: PyTorch for the TCN,
meta-learner, and reconstructor; scikit-learn for the Random Forest; and Scapy together
with \texttt{tshark}/\texttt{editcap}/\texttt{tcpdump} for PCAP handling, feature
realization, and protocol-compliance checking.

The full pipeline is expensive to run end to end, and the cost is worth recording since
it bounds how much exploration a project of this size can afford. Generating the thirty
adversarial captures with TANTGen took $26.7$\,h, dominated by the NTP flood alone
($11.9$\,h, whose raw capture must be streamed to reach the million-packet cap), and
tensorizing the forty sources took a further $11.2$\,h. Of the model stages, the RF
completed its eighteen-point grid search and final fit in $2.7$\,h on CPU (each
configuration fitting in $270$--$522$\,s); the TCN trained for $24.7$\,h on one GPU,
early-stopping after ten of twenty planned epochs with the best epoch at~$6$; the
meta-learner for $3.4$\,h, best epoch $17$ of $20$; and the reconstructor for $10.0$\,h,
best epoch $19$ of $20$. The two comparison studies of \Cref{sec:eval-gradablation} and
\Cref{sec:eval-e2e} cost $4.3$\,h and $33.6$\,h respectively, the latter because it
scores $2{,}704{,}816$ realized flows through the independent detectors. Repeating the
four model stages once per attack, as \Cref{subsec:eval-perattack} does, adds roughly
$28$\,h more.

\section{Metrics}\label{sec:eval-metrics}

The metrics separate into three families.

\paragraph{Static classification.} Treating each scored unit independently at the
end-of-flow (or per-checkpoint) verdict, the evaluation reports \emph{ROC~AUC} and
\emph{PR~AUC} (threshold-free ranking quality; PR~AUC is the more informative summary
under the $\approx\!83\%$ adversarial base rate), \emph{Macro~F1} (the unweighted mean
of the benign and adversarial $F_1$ at threshold $0.5$), and the per-class
\emph{precision} and \emph{recall}. Recall on the adversarial class is reported
separately, because a detector that misses adversarial flows is failing at the one task
it exists for.

\paragraph{Streaming.} A defense that must act before a flow completes cannot be judged
only at end-of-flow. The evaluation therefore reports \emph{AUROC@$t$}: the ROC~AUC
computed using, for each flow, only the prediction available after its first $t$
packets. This exposes how quickly a model becomes discriminative. It is paired with the
\emph{\acf{EDP}}, the median packet index at which a flow's
streaming score first crosses $0.5$, reported with its inter-quartile range, and with
the \emph{detection rate}, the fraction of adversarial flows that ever cross the
threshold. AUROC@$t$ measures ranking quality; EDP and detection rate measure
operational timeliness at a fixed operating point.

\paragraph{Purification.} The reconstructor is judged on four quantities.
\emph{Reconstruction \ac{MSE}} is computed on the feature dimensions TANTGen actually
perturbed, between the flow and its true clean counterpart, measured before (adversarial
input) and after (reconstructor output). The headline efficacy quantity is
\emph{detection restoration through an independent NIDS}: the clean, adversarial, and
purified (or DEPT-defended) flows are scored through the real NIDS each attack was built
to evade, Kitsune or the ML-NIDS, and the \emph{restoration ratio}, the fraction of the
adversarial-to-clean gap recovered, together with the \ac{ASR} before and after DEPT,
report how much detection the defense returns (\Cref{sec:eval-e2e}). A \emph{secondary,
in-distribution diagnostic} is the \emph{purification gain}, the drop in DEPT's own TCN's
adversarial-probability $\Delta P(\text{adv})$ from the adversarial to the reconstructed
flow, with its \emph{residual gap} to a genuinely clean flow; this is circular by
construction (the same detector family the loop trained around) and is read only as a
manifold-return sanity check, not as the end-to-end claim. Finally, \emph{protocol
compliance} is the fraction of realized packets that \texttt{tshark} parses without a
malformed-packet error, computed only over packets the realization step can faithfully
emit.

which is the axis that matches DEPT's task as posed: separate a manipulated flow of a
given source from an unmanipulated flow \emph{of that same source}, rather than from
generic benign traffic drawn from the other nine. It is also the strictest of the three
axes the harness records, and the one on which the system's real failure becomes visible.

$30$ reach $\ge\!0.99$ for the Random Forest and $13$ for the meta-learner. But a

trained. \Cref{sec:eval-discussion} takes up what this means. Two reading caveats: the
per-source figures for the reflection floods rest on the RF's aggregate prefix

Three cross-cutting regularities emerge, and they are not the ones the design
anticipated. First, the \texttt{iat}-only mode is hardest \emph{for the TCN} and easy
for the forest: \texttt{portscan\_iat} ($\text{TCN}\,0.652$ against $\text{RF}\,1.000$),

model whose score carries no information about the label. These floods are one- and

($0.83$--$0.93$ RF, $0.68$--$0.72$ TCN, $0.72$--$0.73$ meta), and it will reappear as the
hardest case for the purifier as well.

The result on this axis is not the one the design predicted, and it is worth stating
plainly before interpreting it. \emph{The Random Forest alone is the strongest stream
at every point from packet~$2$ onward}, reaching $0.9805$ at its first checkpoint and
$0.9813$ thereafter, against the meta-learner's $0.9099$ to $0.9334$ and the TCN's
$0.8225$ to $0.8386$. The meta-learner is not the best model in this system; it is
the second-best, and its advantage over the forest is confined to a single packet.

What the fusion does deliver is precise, and it is a real advantage, but it is
narrower than ``the stack beats its base learners''. It is threefold. First, at
packet~$1$, where the forest is silent by construction, the meta-learner has the best
available verdict of any model, at $0.8373$ against the TCN's $0.8191$; a defense that
must act on the first packet has only these two options, and fusion is the better one.
Second, the meta-learner dominates the TCN at every horizon, by $8.7$ to $9.5$ AUROC
points from packet~$2$ onward, so as a way of using a sequence model it is
unambiguously better than using the sequence model alone. Third, and most consequential
for RQ1, the fused stack is measurably harder to evade than either component under a
gradient-guided attacker, which \Cref{sec:eval-gradablation} quantifies: it is the only
one of the three that keeps $74.5\%$ of flows unevaded at every budget. Robustness, not
clean-traffic accuracy, is what the second base learner buys.

Two further observations complete the picture, and both cut against the fusion. The
meta-learner's per-source behaviour tracks whichever base learner is stronger on that
source, but not reliably: on \texttt{ssdp\_ddos} \texttt{both}/\texttt{iat} it reaches
$0.927$ where the forest reaches $1.000$, and on \texttt{ntp\_ddos}
\texttt{both}/\texttt{iat} it reaches $0.653$--$0.654$ against the forest's $0.999$,
so on those sources it discards most of a signal it was handed. The mechanism is the
one anticipated in \Cref{subsec:sol-meta}: the carry-forward RF logit enters the
per-packet fusion token as a single, sparsely updated feature, refreshed only at
checkpoints and absent before packet~$2$, where a learnable placeholder stands in,
whereas the dense TCN logit is present and updated at every timestep, so the
meta-learner's attention systematically under-weights the intermittent evidence. A stacker should be no
worse than its best base learner; this one is worse on several sources and on the
aggregate, and that is the clearest single target for future work on the detection
stage (\Cref{sec:eval-discussion}).

A note on the auxiliary ``beat the best base'' loss (weight $0.5$,
\Cref{subsec:sol-meta}) closes the section. Over training the auxiliary term falls from
$0.0267$ to $0.0177$, so the meta-learner does move toward matching its best base
learner's confidence; over the same twenty epochs the meta-validation AUROC@$10$ rises
from $0.9197$ to $0.9333$, with essentially all of that gain in the first few epochs and
under $10^{-3}$ of movement after epoch~$10$. The bottleneck is rank ordering, not
calibration: a loss that sharpens confidences cannot by itself recover ranking that the
$13$-dimensional fusion tokens do not support. This negative result is recorded because
it delimits what the auxiliary loss can be expected to do.

\section{Purification Results}\label{sec:eval-purification}

The reconstructor was trained on $1{,}796{,}542$ five-tuple-matched
$(\text{adversarial}\rightarrow\text{clean})$ flow pairs, of $2{,}566{,}489$ paired in
total, and evaluated on the held-out $384{,}963$-pair test split (seed~$42$, unseen by
any base learner). It is an attack-agnostic denoiser: it is trained with uniform-weight
reconstruction over all feature dimensions, so it is given no hint of where TANTGen
perturbed a flow (a perturbation-weighted variant was explored as an ablation during

As with detection, the effect is strongly attack-dependent, and the per-source view is
where the honest reading lives. \Cref{tab:eval-purifsrc} gives the rows that carry the
argument, and \Cref{fig:eval-purifscatter} plots every source--mode pair by its
perturbation footprint (the pre-purification MSE on the perturbed dimensions) against

anticipated and one not.

Purification is large precisely on the structurally perturbed sources: \texttt{ddos}
(gain $0.93$ on \texttt{both}, $0.94$ on \texttt{size}), \texttt{syn\_ddos} ($0.92$ and

inert where TANTGen barely moved the features, chiefly the \texttt{both} and
\texttt{iat} variants of the reflection floods, whose pre-purification MSE is already
near zero. It is unmeasurable on the \texttt{size} variants of those same floods, where
the footprint is maximal ($\text{MSE}=1.0$) and substantially reduced, yet the TCN gain
stays at $\approx\!0$ because that detector assigns adversarial and clean flows the same
probability to begin with: the gain metric is bounded by the detection gap, so on
detector-blind sources it can neither credit nor discredit the purifier.

The fourth regime is the one the earlier framing missed, and it only becomes visible
once an independent judge is used. On sources where TANTGen never succeeded in evading,
purification actively \emph{harms} detection. Through the independent NIDS,
\texttt{dns\_ddos} falls from $0.986$ to $0.762$ on \texttt{both} and from $0.993$ to

\texttt{brute\_force\_size} from $0.993$ to $0.963$. The denoiser is doing what it was
trained to do, moving the flow toward the clean manifold, but on these flows the
attack's fingerprint and the clean manifold were never far apart, so what it removes is
signal the protected NIDS was still using. This is not a defect of the reconstructor so
much as an argument that purification must be \emph{gated} on whether it will help,
which \Cref{sec:eval-e2e} measures end to end and \Cref{sec:eval-discussion} takes up as
a limitation.

Three realization caveats must be stated, and the second and third are worse than in
earlier drafts of this work. First, a size edit on an application-layer \emph{text}
protocol (the SSDP/HTTP \texttt{M-SEARCH} messages in \texttt{ssdp\_flood}
\texttt{both}/\texttt{size}) corrupts the L7 grammar even when the IP and UDP lengths
and checksums remain valid, and \texttt{tshark} flags these as malformed. Rather than
count them as compliance failures, since they are artefacts of byte-overlay realization
on a text protocol and not of the purifier, the $450{,}758$ such packets are reported
separately as unrealizable at L7, and the $97.22\%$ rate is computed over the
$2{,}412{,}357$ realizable ones. For \texttt{ssdp\_flood} \texttt{size} that exclusion is
total: every packet of that stream is L7-unrealizable, so it has no compliance or
independent-NIDS figure at all. Second, the aggregate compliance rate is pulled down by
two sources, \texttt{ddos} at $0.84$ (of $140{,}128$ realized packets, $22{,}623$ are
flagged, mostly length and checksum corner cases at scale) and \texttt{syn\_ddos}
\texttt{iat} at $0.178$; every other source is at or above $0.99$. Third, the
\texttt{syn\_ddos} \texttt{iat} figure rests on a thin and unrepresentative slice, since
only $927$ of that source's $10{,}748$ test pairs survive the equal-length pairing guard,
and of the $2{,}088$ packets realized from them $1{,}716$ carry a bad TCP checksum after
the rewrite while none is malformed, which points at the realization step for that slice
rather than at the reconstructor. None of these is a claim about purification quality,
but they do bound how far the traffic-space results can be pushed.

To make these aggregates concrete, it is worth following a single real packet through
the pipeline. \Cref{fig:eval-purifpacket} traces one flow drawn from the held-out test
split, a four-packet Mirai SYN-flood burst from \texttt{192.168.2.108:35220} to
\texttt{52.25.66.250:8280} under the \texttt{size} variant. In the original capture each
datagram is $44$~bytes, a $20$-byte IPv4 header followed by a $24$-byte TCP header with
no application payload. TANTGen appends $29$~bytes of payload to every packet, inflating
the IPv4 total-length field to $73$~bytes; this length shift is the whole of the
perturbation the \texttt{size} variant applies, and it is what pushes the flow off the
clean manifold. The reconstructor recovers the original length, and the traffic-space
realization of \Cref{subsec:sol-realization} trims the appended bytes back off, returning
every datagram to $44$~bytes, a residual of exactly zero on the manipulated field. All
twelve packets across the three stages pass \texttt{tshark} dissection with no malformed
frames and no bad checksums, and because the \texttt{size} variant perturbs length alone
the inter-arrival times are identical between the clean and adversarial captures, so
packet size is the only quantity that changes. The $17.0\times$ reduction in
perturbed-dimension MSE and the restored detection reported above are, at the
granularity of one packet, this $44 \rightarrow 73 \rightarrow 44$ restoration.

\section{Ensemble Robustness under a Gradient-guided Attacker (RQ1)}\label{sec:eval-gradablation}

% NOTE: numbers in this section come from
% data/models/gradient_ablation/gradient_ablation_metrics.json
% (scripts/gradient_ablation/run_gradient_ablation.py), full-scale run of 2026-07-20:
% 9,000 flows = 300 per source x 30 sources, 6 budgets, policy fpr_0.05, best-of
% (PGD u random) attacker under the monotone envelope.

Every result so far was produced against TANTGen, and TANTGen cannot answer RQ1. It is
black-box and gradient-free, so it exercises no gradient path; measuring it tells us the
ensemble detects \emph{its} perturbations, not what the ensemble's heterogeneity is worth
against an adversary that differentiates. This section supplies that adversary.

\subsection{The Attacker}\label{subsec:eval-gradablation-attacker}

The attacker is given white-box access to the \ac{TCN} (architecture, weights and
gradients) and nothing for the Random Forest, whose non-differentiability means there is
no gradient to withhold. It crafts \emph{one} perturbation against the \ac{TCN}, using
\ac{PGD}~\cite{madry2018towards}, and that same perturbation is then replayed, unchanged,
through each target's own featurisation and scored. This one-perturbation-three-targets
discipline is what makes the comparison a comparison: were the attacker allowed to
re-optimize per target, it would no longer be measuring transfer.

The perturbation is \emph{increase-only} padding and delay, per packet, applied on top of
TANTGen's own, so the cost reported is the \emph{extra} budget an attacker must spend. The
padding bytes are drawn at random (\texttt{attack.pad\_byte\_mode}); this is not a neutral
detail, because zero and random padding move the Random Forest's payload-entropy features,
its largest feature group, in opposite directions, while the \ac{TCN}'s gradient sees no
payload bytes and so cannot distinguish the two.
Budgets are denominated in bytes and seconds rather than in an $\ell_\infty$ ball, and
this is forced rather than stylistic: the \ac{IPAT} channel is standardized with a
standard deviation of $211{,}047$~seconds while the $1088$ nPrint channels are bits in
$\{-1,0,1\}$, so a single $\epsilon = 0.01$ would buy the attacker ${\pm}2110$~seconds of
delay and nothing whatever in the bit channels. The feasible set (increase-only, integer
bytes, capped at the \ac{MTU}, inert on non-IPv4 packets) is enforced as a projection.

Three controls guard the result, because a defense evaluated with a weak attack flatters
itself~\cite{carlini2019evaluating, tramer2020adaptive}. First, a \emph{query-matched
random search}: the same feasible set and budget, an equal number of forward passes, and
no gradient. Second, \emph{AutoPGD}~\cite{croce2020reliable} via the \ac{ART}
library~\cite{nicolae2018adversarial}, a parameter-free third-party optimizer, as an
independent check that the search is not simply under-tuned; it agrees, finding the same
$100\%$ evasion of the \ac{TCN} at the same budget. Third, the reported attacker is the
\emph{best of} \ac{PGD} and random search per flow, so no result rests on a favourite
optimizer.

\subsection{How the Numbers Must Be Read}\label{subsec:eval-gradablation-reading}

Two conventions are load-bearing, and both cut against the defense rather than for it.

\ac{ASR} is measured \emph{only over the flows a target detects unperturbed}. This is the
standard robust-accuracy convention, and here it is the difference between a measurement
and an artifact: of the $9{,}000$ sampled flows, the \ac{TCN} detects only $3{,}374$ at
its matched threshold, against $7{,}510$ for the forest and $7{,}597$ for the stack, so an
unconditional \ac{ASR} would mostly be counting flows the detector never caught. A target
that does not detect a flow cannot be evaded on it.

Thresholds are set at a \emph{matched false-positive rate} on unperturbed benign traffic,
never at a raw $0.5$. The three targets are calibrated very differently, with median
verdicts on benign traffic of $0.043$ for the forest, $0.379$ for the \ac{TCN}, and
$0.002$ for the stack, so a fixed threshold would reward whichever model happens to say
``adversarial'' least often. At a common $5\%$ false-positive rate the thresholds are
$0.4535$, $0.9820$, and $0.3658$ respectively.

Finally, the curves are reported as a monotone envelope over the budget grid. The feasible
sets are nested, so an attacker permitted $1460$~bytes may simply spend $128$; a rational
attacker's success cannot fall as its budget grows, and any measured dip is the optimizer
faltering rather than the defense strengthening.

\paragraph{The stack costs the attacker more than either base learner alone, at every
budget an attacker would plausibly pay.} This is RQ1's answer. At the three smallest
non-zero budgets the fused stack concedes $1.7\%$, $4.7\%$, and $8.5\%$ attack success
against the forest's $8.6\%$, $10.7\%$, and $13.1\%$ and the \ac{TCN}'s $40.5\%$,
$50.1\%$, and $52.2\%$; across the whole grid $74.5\%$ of flows never evade the stack at
any budget, against $64.0\%$ for the forest and $45.5\%$ for the \ac{TCN}, whose median
evading flow needs only the smallest budget on the grid. The perturbation that breaks the
\ac{TCN} it was crafted against transfers poorly to the forest, and the fusion of the two
is harder than either. For scale, TANTGen's own edits average about $29$ to $32$ padding
bytes per packet on the sources where it perturbs most, which places a realistic attacker
in the $32$ to $128$ byte region of the grid, where the stack concedes at most $8.5\%$.

One qualification belongs in the same breath. The ordering is not uniform across the
whole grid: at $512$ bytes the stack ($18.9\%$) and the forest ($18.4\%$) are level, and
the stack pulls ahead again only at $1460$ bytes ($25.5\%$ against $36.0\%$). The
defensible claim is therefore that the stack strictly dominates the \ac{TCN} everywhere
and the forest everywhere except one grid point, not that it dominates both everywhere.

\paragraph{The meta-learner shifts its reliance onto the Random Forest as the TCN is
degraded.} This is the design claim of \Cref{subsec:sol-meta} stated as a falsifiable
prediction, and it survives the test. Ablating the meta-learner's two base-learner token
slots, and measuring how far its verdict moves when each is handed back its clean value,
the share attributable to the forest goes from $0.000$ at $\delta = 0$, where there is
nothing to compensate for, to $0.529$ as soon as the attacker spends anything, rising to
$0.563$ at $128$ bytes and staying between $0.54$ and $0.56$ thereafter, with between
$5{,}869$ and $6{,}458$ of the $9{,}000$ flows registering a moved verdict. The stack does
lean on the learner the attacker cannot differentiate. The behaviour is visible even
before any attack: where the \ac{TCN} is blind at $\delta = 0$ and the forest is not, as
on \texttt{ldap\_ddos}, \texttt{ntp\_ddos} \texttt{both}/\texttt{iat},
\texttt{portscan\_iat} and \texttt{syn\_ddos\_iat}, the stack inherits the forest's
detection of $\approx\!1.00$. It also inherits the forest's failures, reading
$\approx\!0.00$ wherever the forest does, as on \texttt{ssdp\_ddos\_size} and
\texttt{ntp\_ddos\_size}, and it is not uniformly the harder target per source: on
\texttt{ntp\_ddos\_both} and \texttt{ssdp\_ddos\_both} the stack is easier to evade at
$128$ bytes than the forest alone ($20\%$ and $43\%$ against $1\%$), because its fused
verdict sits closer to its own threshold than the forest's does to its.

\paragraph{Gradients buy this attacker nothing at all.} Query-matched random search does
not merely match \ac{PGD} on the \ac{TCN}, it beats it at every budget, by
$40.1\%$ against $37.2\%$ at $8$ bytes and $52.8\%$ against $50.7\%$ at $1460$. The cause
is representational rather than a failure of tuning: nPrint encodes packet size as $16$
binary total-length columns, so $255 \to 256$ flips nine of them at once and neighbouring
byte counts sit far apart in the model's input space. The loss surface over packet size is
correspondingly jagged, and a descent direction computed there carries little information.
\ac{ART}'s AutoPGD confirms the search is not simply under-tuned: on the relaxed problem
all three attackers push every one of the sampled flows past the decision boundary, with
median margins of $-0.79$ to $-0.85$. This is a property of the \emph{representation} the
defense happens to use, not a robustness claim, and it bounds the RQ1 result in a specific
way: what has been demonstrated is that adding a non-differentiable component raises
evasion cost against a strong search-based attacker, not that gradient-based attacks are
weak in general.

\section{End-to-End System Evaluation}\label{sec:eval-e2e}

\subsection{Detect-then-Purify, End to End}\label{subsec:eval-e2e-detect}

Composing the stages, the end-to-end effect of DEPT can be measured either through the
protected detector's own verdicts or through an independent one. The internal view is
reported first, and briefly, because it is the weaker of the two: through DEPT's own TCN,
the mean $P(\text{adv})$ on a purified flow falls from $0.675$ to $0.428$ against a
clean-flow level of $0.421$, so purification returns flagged flows to the benign
operating point as that detector scores it. On the structurally perturbed sources this
is the difference between a near-certain detection ($P(\text{adv})\approx0.99$ on
\texttt{portscan} and \texttt{ddos}) and a near-certain pass ($\approx0.06$--$0.15$).

These figures are measured through DEPT's own TCN standing in for the protected NIDS,
which makes them an \emph{internal} view: they show that purification returns a flow to
the manifold as scored by the same family of detector the loop was trained around, but
they cannot, on their own, rule out circularity. The credible end-to-end question is
different, and this thesis answers it directly rather than deferring it: what is
TANTGen's attack-success rate against the \emph{independent} NIDS each attack was
actually built to evade, before and after DEPT is placed in front of it?

\Cref{tab:eval-baseline} reports that deployment baseline. For every attack, three
realized full-volume streams are scored through its true target NIDS: \textbf{Kitsune}
(KitNET~+~AfterImage) for the two Kitsune-native attacks, and TANTGen's own
\textbf{ML-NIDS} (the per-attack CICFlowMeter flow classifiers) for the eight
CIC-IDS2017 and CIC-DDoS-2019 attacks, reusing in each case the very model TANTGen
calibrated its evasion against, so that no DEPT component is ever asked to grade itself.

flags each flow and only the flagged flows are purified while the rest pass through
unchanged, so that detection should climb back from the floor toward the ceiling. The
three streams are realized onto the same adversarial packets, differing only in the size
and timing the purifier touches (\Cref{subsec:sol-realization}), which makes the
comparison a controlled one. The headline quantities are the attack-success rate (one

ratio, the fraction of the adversarial-to-clean detection gap the pipeline recovers.

alone it flatters the system, because the average is taken over thirty source--mode
combinations whose individual outcomes differ in kind rather than in degree.

\Cref{fig:eval-baseline} makes the structure plain, and it is worth stating in three
parts. \emph{The gain is real and large where TANTGen actually evaded.} On
\texttt{ddos} the pipeline removes $38$ points of attack success and on \texttt{portscan}
$32$, and at mode granularity the strongest cases are \texttt{ddos\_size} ($+67.4$
points), \texttt{portscan\_both} ($+67.0$), \texttt{ddos\_both} ($+59.7$), and
\texttt{portscan\_size} ($+46.8$). These are exactly the structural
\texttt{both}/\texttt{size} edits that the per-source purification results predicted
would be recoverable. \emph{Most combinations are untouched because there was nothing to
recover}: on \texttt{ldap\_ddos}, \texttt{ntp\_ddos}, \texttt{ssdp\_ddos} and
\texttt{brute\_force}, TANTGen never evaded in the first place (adversarial detection is
already $0.98$ to $1.00$), so the pipeline moves the number by less than a point in
either direction. \emph{And on a minority of combinations the defense makes matters
worse.} \texttt{dns\_ddos} loses $22.5$ points on \texttt{both} and $22.7$ on
\texttt{iat}, \texttt{portscan\_iat} loses $18.2$, \texttt{ddos\_iat} loses $12.2$, and
\texttt{mirai\_size} loses $23.9$. The mechanism is the one \Cref{sec:eval-purification}
identified: the gate flags essentially all flood traffic, so flows the NIDS was still
catching get purified, and purification removes signal it was using.

The gate's behaviour is the proximate cause and is worth quantifying, since it is what a
practitioner would tune first. Its flag rate is close to $100\%$ on every flood source
and close to zero on several \texttt{size} variants ($0.0\%$ on \texttt{ssdp\_ddos},
$0.2\%$ on \texttt{ntp\_ddos}, $0.8\%$ on \texttt{ldap\_ddos}, $0.9\%$ on
\texttt{dns\_ddos}), which is the same reflection-flood \texttt{size} blind spot the base
learners showed in \Cref{tab:eval-persource}, now visible in the deployed pipeline. One
consequence is that the gated pipeline and an ungated one that purifies everything are
nearly indistinguishable here: the \texttt{purified\_all} upper bound reaches $0.8831$
detection against the gated $0.8836$. The gate as built is therefore not doing much work,
and the finding that matters is not that the gate is imperfect but that it is answering
the wrong question. It predicts \emph{whether a flow is adversarial}; what the pipeline
needs is a predictor of \emph{whether purifying this flow will help}, which on this
evidence is a different quantity.

Split by detector family, the two halves diverge: the eight ML-NIDS sources gain $9.9$
points of \ac{ASR} reduction over $2{,}622{,}023$ flows, while the two Kitsune sources
lose $1.7$ points over $82{,}793$. The Kitsune figure rests almost entirely on
\texttt{mirai\_size}, where TANTGen's size edit had inadvertently \emph{raised} Kitsune's
detection from $0.003$ to $0.242$ and DEPT removed that self-sabotage, returning it to
$0.003$. This is the purifier working correctly and the defense losing by it, which is
an instructive combination rather than a contradiction.

Two methodological notes accompany these numbers. First, because the defended stream runs
DEPT's detector and purifier over all of an attack's flows for full-volume realism, some
of those flows were seen in training; restricted to the held-out test flows the defended
detection rate is $0.9061$ over $372{,}321$ flows, slightly above the full-stream
$0.8836$, so the deployment view is not flattered by the training overlap. Second,
Kitsune is a partly volumetric detector, so its detection rate is only meaningful on full
streams such as these, which is exactly why the baseline feeds full realized traffic
rather than a flow subset; the reconstructor evaluation of \Cref{sec:eval-purification},
which by construction sees only the held-out test pairs, therefore leads with the
scale-robust restoration ratio instead, while the flow-level ML-NIDS is unaffected either
way. With this baseline the independent-NIDS validation of RQ2 that earlier drafts had
left open is delivered rather than deferred, and \Cref{sec:eval-discussion} discusses
what it does and does not settle.

confidently wrong ($0.443$ against $0.192$ on \texttt{dns\_ddos}), not right.

requires no routing and never loses catastrophically, while the timing-only modes of
high-rate floods would justify a specialist where that traffic matters. The
reflection-flood \texttt{size} blind spot appears in both regimes, which localizes it in
the feature representation rather than in the training scope.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:eval-discussion}

\paragraph{Answering the research questions.} RQ1 is answered affirmatively by
measurement rather than by design argument: an adversary handed the \ac{TCN}'s gradients,
and nothing for the forest because a forest has none to give, pays more to evade the
fused stack than to evade either component at every budget it would plausibly spend, and
$74.5\%$ of flows never evade the stack at all against $45.5\%$ for the \ac{TCN}
(\Cref{sec:eval-gradablation}), within the boundaries set out in
\Cref{subsec:eval-gradablation}. RQ2 is answered affirmatively but conditionally: through
the independent NIDS each attack was built to evade, purification recovers $63.0\%$ of
the detection TANTGen destroyed, and $99.9\%$ of it on the Kitsune-native sources where
the evasion had actually succeeded, while emitting $97.2\%$-compliant packets. The
condition is that this holds \emph{where the attack left a footprint the protected
detector could see}; where it did not, purification ranges from inert to actively
harmful, which \Cref{sec:eval-e2e} quantifies and the next paragraph takes up. The
wall-clock cost of the pipeline, its inference latency and overhead, is not measured in
this thesis; that evaluation is deferred to future work (\Cref{chap:conclusion}).

\paragraph{Purification without a good gate can subtract as well as add.} This is the
most consequential negative finding of this evaluation, and it did not appear until the
defense was scored end to end through an independent detector. On the sources where
TANTGen never actually evaded, purifying the flow \emph{lowers} the protected NIDS's
detection, by $22.5$ and $22.7$ points on \texttt{dns\_ddos} \texttt{both} and
\texttt{iat}, $18.2$ on \texttt{portscan\_iat}, and $12.2$ on \texttt{ddos\_iat}. The
denoiser is behaving correctly by its own objective, moving the flow toward the clean
manifold; the problem is that on those flows the attack's fingerprint and the clean
manifold were never far apart, so what it removes is signal the NIDS was still using. The
gate is supposed to prevent this and does not, because it flags nearly all flood traffic:
the gated pipeline and an ungated one that purifies everything reach $0.8836$ and
$0.8831$ detection respectively, which is to say the gate is barely gating. The diagnosis
is sharper than ``the gate needs tuning''. The gate predicts whether a flow is
adversarial, whereas what the pipeline needs is a predictor of whether purification will
improve this particular flow's detectability, and those are different quantities. Until
that is fixed, the aggregate $9.5$-point \ac{ASR} reduction should be read as a net of
large gains on six source--mode combinations against real losses on five others.

\paragraph{The reflection floods' \texttt{size} variants fail below chance, and that is
a representation problem.} On \texttt{dns\_ddos}, \texttt{ldap\_ddos}, \texttt{ntp\_ddos}
and \texttt{ssdp\_ddos} the \texttt{size} variants score $0.213$ to $0.476$ for the
Random Forest and $0.443$ to $0.497$ for the meta-learner against same-source benign
flows (\Cref{tab:eval-persource}): worse than chance, meaning a TANTGen-edited flow of
these floods resembles generic benign traffic \emph{more} than that flood's own unedited
flows do. Three pieces of evidence locate the cause in the feature representation rather
than in capacity or tuning. The per-attack specialists fail on exactly the same slices
(\Cref{tab:eval-perattack}), so it is not a matter of training scope. The purifier
reduces the footprint on these sources substantially ($1.000 \rightarrow 0.302$ on
\texttt{dns\_ddos\_size}) without changing any detector's verdict, so the information is
present in the tensor but not in a form the models use. And the same sources' deployed
gate flags under $1\%$ of their flows, so the failure propagates coherently through the
whole pipeline. What DEPT's $24$ prefix statistics and $1088$ header bits cannot express
is whatever distinguishes a padded single-response UDP flood from an unpadded one, and
identifying that is the natural next experiment.

\paragraph{A stacker that is beaten by its own base learner.} The meta-learner's
flow-level AUROC of $0.9334$ sits below the Random Forest's $0.9813$ at every horizon
from packet~$2$ onward (\Cref{tab:eval-streaming}), and on individual sources the gap is
much wider, $0.653$ against $0.999$ on \texttt{ntp\_ddos} \texttt{both}/\texttt{iat} and
$0.927$ against $1.000$ on \texttt{ssdp\_ddos}. A stacker should be no worse than its
best base learner; this one is worse in aggregate and on several sources. The mechanism
is identified in \Cref{sec:eval-meta}, namely that the sparsely refreshed forest logit is
systematically under-weighted next to the dense \ac{TCN} logit, and the per-attack
comparison adds supporting evidence, since the specialist stacks show the same pathology
more severely (on \texttt{mirai}, a specialist meta-learner at $0.705$ against its own
forest at $0.889$). What the fusion \emph{does} buy is genuine but narrower than the
design claimed: the only packet-$1$ verdict available, an improvement of nearly ten AUROC
points over using the \ac{TCN} alone, and the evasion robustness of
\Cref{sec:eval-gradablation}. Redesigning the fusion so that it can fall back to its best
base learner is the clearest single improvement available to this system.

\paragraph{Purification is validated through independent NIDS, not only DEPT's own
detector.} The purification gain and residual gap of \Cref{sec:eval-purification} are
computed with the TCN as the protected NIDS. That is the correct diagnostic for ``did the
flow return to the manifold the pipeline was trained around'', but it is circular, and it
is also why that gain metric goes silent exactly on the sources the TCN cannot separate.
The deployment baseline of \Cref{subsec:eval-e2e-detect} closes this gap: it scores the
clean, adversarial, and DEPT-defended streams through the \emph{independent} NIDS each
attack was built to evade, Kitsune for the two Kitsune-native attacks and TANTGen's
ML-NIDS for the eight CIC attacks, so the end-to-end claim no longer rests on DEPT
grading itself. Two residual caveats remain, and they are methodological rather than
open questions. First, the defended full-stream figures run DEPT's models over flows some
of which appeared in training, so they are reported alongside a held-out-test breakdown
(\Cref{subsec:eval-e2e-detect}). Second, Kitsune's detection rate is partly volumetric
and is therefore trustworthy only on the full streams the baseline feeds, not on a flow
subset; the flow-level ML-NIDS is not subject to this. Neither caveat reintroduces the
circularity the TCN-only metric carried.

\paragraph{The label convention is a modelling choice.} Treating every TANTGen flow as
adversarial and every raw-capture flow as benign makes the target precise (``did
TANTGen touch this flow''), but it also means that a source TANTGen barely perturbs is
essentially unlabelable through no fault of the detector. The
\texttt{both} and \texttt{iat} variants of the reflection floods are the clearest case,
with a pre-purification footprint of $\text{MSE}\approx0$ indicating that the generator
moved almost nothing; the models nevertheless score $\approx\!1.00$ there, so the
convention is not by itself fatal. Any reader of the aggregate numbers should hold this
framing in mind all the same, since it determines what ``adversarial'' means throughout.

\paragraph{What the aggregates do and do not summarize.} Three headline numbers appear
above, and each is an average over a distribution that is not unimodal. The
meta-learner's $0.9334$ AUROC averages $13$ source--mode combinations at or above $0.99$
with four below chance. The $63.0\%$ purification restoration averages $99.9\%$ on the
sources where evasion succeeded with near-zero on the many where there was nothing to
restore. The $9.5$-point end-to-end \ac{ASR} reduction nets gains of $47$ to $67$ points
on four combinations, and $11$ to $20$ on two more, against losses of $12$ to $24$ on
five. Reporting the per-source
tables alongside is not a hedge but the substance: a defense that helps enormously on
some traffic and hurts on other traffic is a different object from one that helps
moderately everywhere, and only the disaggregated view distinguishes them.

\subsection{Scope of the Gradient-ablation Result (RQ1)}\label{subsec:eval-gradablation}

\Cref{sec:eval-gradablation} answers RQ1 by measurement, but three boundaries on that answer
deserve stating plainly, because each of them limits what the numbers license.

\paragraph{RQ1 is only answerable where the base learners detect anything.} An attack
cannot evade a detector that never caught the flow, so the \ac{ASR} denominator is the
flows a target detects unperturbed, and on several sources that denominator is thin or
empty. At $\delta = 0$ the \ac{TCN} detects between nothing and $2\%$ of flows on
\texttt{brute\_force}, \texttt{ldap\_ddos}, \texttt{mirai} \texttt{both}/\texttt{iat},
\texttt{ntp\_ddos}, \texttt{portscan\_iat}, \texttt{ssdp\_ddos} and
\texttt{syn\_ddos\_iat}, and on those sources its per-source \ac{ASR} is undefined
because the denominator is empty. Flow length compounds it: \texttt{ldap\_ddos},
\texttt{portscan}, \texttt{ssdp\_ddos} and \texttt{syn\_ddos} have a median of two
packets, against a \ac{TCN} whose receptive field is $29$. The forest's denominator is
much healthier, near $1.00$ everywhere except the four reflection-flood \texttt{size}
variants, so the comparison is real; but the \ac{TCN} arm of it is carried by the
minority of sources where the sequence model has both a stream to read and a signal to
lose, and it should not be read as a statement about the corpus as a whole.

\paragraph{Some flows are unreachable through this surface at any budget.} On
\texttt{ddos} \texttt{both}/\texttt{size}, \texttt{portscan} \texttt{both}/\texttt{size},
and \texttt{ssdp\_flood\_size} both the forest and the stack hold the attacker to $0\%$
success through $1460$~bytes per packet. That is not robustness in any useful sense. It
says the two perturbable physical quantities cannot express a change large enough to
overcome that much model confidence, and a different attack surface, or a different
attacker, would be needed to say anything about those flows.

\paragraph{The timing channel has a representational floor.} A delay smaller than
${\approx}3.07\,\mu$s is one float32 ULP of the standardized \ac{IPAT} channel and simply
does not survive into the tensor. \texttt{dns\_ddos}'s median inter-arrival time
($2.38\,\mu$s) sits \emph{below} that floor. Where timing-mode results look flat, the
representation is a candidate explanation before the architecture is, and disentangling
the two is left to future work.

\paragraph{The attacker that was priced is not a gradient attacker in the end.} Because
query-matched random search beats \ac{PGD} on the \ac{TCN} at every budget, what
\Cref{sec:eval-gradablation} actually measures is the cost to a strong \emph{search-based}
attacker with white-box access to half the ensemble. That is still the right comparison
for RQ1, since it isolates exactly the asymmetry the question asks about, but the
mechanism is not the one the question presumes. On nPrint's binary size encoding a
gradient is worth less than a restart budget, so a reader should take from this that a
non-differentiable component raises evasion cost, not that gradient attacks are
intrinsically weak against stacked ensembles.

Set against these, the measured result is a genuine but bounded one: on the sources where
the question is well-posed, a single perturbation crafted against the \ac{TCN} with its
gradients costs the attacker materially more against the stack than against either
component at realistic budgets, $74.5\%$ of flows never evade the stack at all, and the
meta-learner's reliance demonstrably migrates toward the learner the attacker cannot
differentiate.
