\noindent Machine-learning-based network intrusion detectors can be evaded by adversaries who subtly manipulate network traffic (e.g., by changing packet sizes or inter-packet arrival times), while preserving protocol compliance and the ability to achieve their attack objectives.
However, many state-of-the-art defenses are evaluated using feature perturbations that may not translate into realizable network traffic, limiting their practical applicability.
This thesis proposes DEPT, a streaming defense that detects and purifies manipulated traffic before it reaches a protected detector.

DEPT represents traffic as masked packet-level bits and flow statistics at two temporal granularities.
For each packet, an attention-based meta-learner combines a Random Forest and a Temporal Convolutional Network to identify manipulated flows.
Flagged flows are corrected in feature space using a causal purification Transformer, with the corrected features mapped back to packet sizes and timings. .
Training and evaluation rely on TANTGen~\cite{pissarra2026tantgen}, a black-box adversarial traffic generator, covering ten traffic sources from three public datasets and three perturbation modes.

Experimental results show that DEPT achieves an end-of-flow AUROC of $0.933$ in detecting adversarial manipulations.
Although the Random Forest alone outperforms the ensemble from the second packet onwards, the ensemble enables detection from the very first packet and provides greater robustness.
Purification restores $63\%$ of the detection lost to adversarial manipulation, reducing attack success from $21.1\%$ to $11.6\%$.
In contrast, a state-of-the-art diffusion-based purifier~\cite{merzouk2024diffusion} recovers almost none of the lost detection.
Moreover, $97.2\%$ of DEPT's generated packets parse without malformed-packet errors, supporting the practical feasibility of the approach.
Nevertheless, performance varies across traffic source, with manipulation detection struggling on size-perturbed reflection floods, and purification occasionally reducing detection performance on flows that had not successfully evaded the detector.
