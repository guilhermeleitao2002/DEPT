\noindent Machine-learning-based network intrusion detectors are vulnerable to adversarial evasion. An attacker that
reshapes only packet timing and sizes, while preserving protocol compliance and attack functionality, can make
an ongoing attack look benign to a deployed detector. Much of the defensive literature
measures robustness against feature perturbations without establishing whether they correspond to realizable packet sequences. This
thesis proposes DEPT (Defense via Ensemble and Purification Transformer), a streaming
defense that detects adversarial manipulation and purifies suspicious traffic before it reaches a protected intrusion detector.

DEPT represents traffic using masked packet-level bits and aggregated flow statistics at two temporal granularities. 
An attention-based meta-learner combines the predictions of two architecturally different
learners, a Random Forest and a Temporal Convolutional Network, at each packet, to detect adversarially manipulated flows.
A causal purification Transformer then corrects flagged flows in feature space, and a reconstruction stage applies these corrections to packet timing and size, projecting them back into traffic-space.

DEPT is trained and evaluated on adversarial traffic generated by TANTGen~\cite{pissarra2026tantgen}, a black-box generator, across ten traffic sources from three public datasets, each manipulated in three perturbation modes.
DEPT produces predictions from the first packet and reaches an end-of-flow area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of $0.933$ for manipulation detection. 
Although the Random Forest alone achieves higher AUROC from the second packet onwards, the ensemble provides earlier predictions and greater resistance against the evaluated attacks targeting the sequence model.
Evaluated against the independent detector each attack targeted, purification recovers $63\%$ of the detection-rate loss caused by the adversarial manipulation.
In the full-pipeline evaluation, DEPT reduces attack success from $21.1\%$ to $11.6\%$ and outperforms the diffusion-based purifier of Merzouk et al.~\cite{merzouk2024diffusion}, which, under the same conditions, recovers almost none of the detection lost to evasion.
Among packets classified as realizable, $97.2\%$ are parsed without malformed-packet errors. 
However, effectiveness varies across traffic sources: manipulation detection remains near or below chance on size-perturbed reflection floods, and purification can reduce downstream detection when manipulation has not caused evasion.
